## Verification and exit

The first slice's proposed synthetic acceptance example has 80 founder hours in a month, including 16 administration and 8 business-development hours. Stream A allocates 32 hours at 8 hours per delivered unit; stream B allocates 24 hours at 8 hours per unit. Founder capacity is therefore four A units and three B units, not seven units for each stream. With one separately costed provider unit available for each stream, requested volumes of five A units at $1,000 and four B units at $600 yield $7,400 revenue and $500 contractor expense ($300 plus $200). A separately excluded $2,000 offer contributes nothing to that base case. Overallocating founder hours must report the conflict, not silently choose a winning stream. Missing hours or unit conversions must prevent a verified capacity result; an explicitly zero capacity must produce zero deliverable volume. These are proposed independent test expectations, not observations about Tania or completed test results.

Use independently calculated fixture expectations, not snapshots of the implementation. Tests cover missing versus zero, calendar boundaries/leap years, decimal precision, zero/negative contribution, shared-resource overcommitment, deposits and beyond-horizon AR/AP, owner-pay downward responses, tax losses without invented refunds, actuals immutability, late/duplicate/restricted funding, scenario input effectiveness and full cash/profit bridges. F1 must retain its original observations and unknowns; F2 is not a synthetic substitute for approved current inputs.

Run focused unit tests per calculation slice; PostgreSQL feature tests for persistence/revision/audit/permissions; regression tests for current review continuity and both direct/in-app entrepreneur navigation; static, methodology and structural guards; then full CI and browser checks for integrated changes. Keep repository coverage floors at 85% overall, 80% per module and 90% critical. Inspect representative primary/fallback PDFs when financial rendering changes.

Phase 2 is complete only when the integrated versioned engine, all required scenarios, metrics, actuals handover and nine new rule adapters meet the specification. A foundation PR or green CI alone does not establish that outcome. F2 reconstruction cannot be completed without its agreed inputs, and Release-certified remains a later programme milestone.

## Slice 2A implementation record

`Forecast/RevenueProjection` is a standalone, versioned revenue projection with explicit calendar months and immutable stream/month/founder input contracts. Decimal arithmetic caps delivered units by allocated founder hours plus costed provider capacity. Both preparation and delivery consume time; administration and business development consume the same shared founder budget. Base totals exclude upside offers, and unknown membership prevents a known base total. Upside rows are individual potential projections, not proof that they can run simultaneously with the base; including one requires a fresh shared-capacity calculation.

Missing months, prices, capacity, units, evidence status, bookings and invoicing remain visible. Known delivery arithmetic can remain visible when a separate booking/invoice input is missing, but the monthly result is Cannot verify. Explicit zero capacity produces zero delivery. Overallocated founder time and zero hours-per-unit produce Fail, retain any separate missing inputs, and suppress unusable base totals. Negative/malformed decimal inputs, duplicate IDs and out-of-calendar schedules are rejected instead of coerced or silently discarded. The monetary policy rounds capacity down to eight decimal places, rounds each stream-month monetary line half up to cents, then sums those lines. Input hashes are insensitive to stream/map ordering and change with actual input/evidence changes.

This slice enables **no additional gate rules**. It supplies tested calculation inputs for future PM-05/06/11 adapters. Existing stored budgets, save/recalculate routes, forecasts and F1 are unchanged; the new engine is not selectable through the application yet. This is not a complete cash model, an evidence-verification decision or F2. The existing broad entrepreneur-service dependency hash includes new service files, so deploying this foundation can invalidate recorded release-risk dependencies even though it does not recalculate client budgets.

The synthetic `tests/Fixtures/Entrepreneurs/Forecast/revenue-drivers.json` independently expects $7,400 base revenue, $500 contractor expense and 80 allocated founder hours, with a separate excluded $2,000 potential line. It is not a substitute for approved F2 inputs. Critical coverage lists every new engine/contract file at the unchanged 90% threshold. ## Slice 2D implementation plan

Checked on 6 November 2026 against merged Phase 2C `fcc5f981` (verified in production as 1.0.285) and the settlement guard `453f5035` from PR #174. Storage, gate, PDF and workflow boundaries in "Current code and integration boundaries" are unchanged. It does not persist scenarios, select a stored model, enable gate rules or size a real facility for a client.

- Define the six required scenarios as explicit named definitions, not hard-coded multipliers at the call site. Base, revenue downside, slow start, cost upside, collections delay and combined downside each record their shock type, factor, eligible target set and the configured defaults they came from. A scenario that cannot state its eligible targets is Cannot verify, not an unshocked pass.
- Report requested and effective shock separately for every scenario. A requested volume factor of 0.8 whose effective revenue reduction differs after capacity limits and pricing must show both figures and the reason. Slow start applies to the first three forecast months after the actuals cutoff only, then the plan ramp resumes.
- Protect actuals. Every shock resolves its eligible months through `ActualsWindow::assertForecastPeriods()` before any recomputation. Elapsed reviewed months keep their values in every scenario, and an attempt to shock an actual month fails rather than silently clipping. Cost upside recomputes tax and dependent responses; it never multiplies tax, financing receipts or historical actuals.
- Never force monotonicity. A lower-volume scenario may legitimately improve an early cash minimum through lower supplier payments or tax; the bridge and any profit deterioration are reported as computed. The worst-case scenario is selected by an explicitly named ranking metric, recorded alongside the result.
- Produce the lender metrics as typed results with their own stated bases: Month 12-13 bridge by stream, break-even units and utilisation, no-sales expense cover, forecast cash runway, owner pay as a percentage of revenue, and same-period DSCR. Break-even requires a positive contribution margin and a declared unit; zero or negative contribution returns no finite break-even under that case, not zero units. Multi-stream break-even requires an explicit sales mix. DSCR with no debt service returns Not applicable, never zero, infinity or a fabricated capacity, and carries its debt amount, rate, draw dates, term, fees, repayment schedule and any interest-only period. Runway that never goes negative within the horizon reports horizon coverage only, not an indefinite runway.
- Compute the funding measures per scenario in the specification's order and keep them separable: dated unrestricted closing cash before any proposed facility, cash deficit as `max(0, -min_t(C))`, reserve target as three months of fixed costs plus the 10% contingency on that basis and identified one-off cash costs, and reserve-target shortfall as `max(0, max_t(reserve_target - C))`. The shortfall already includes any cash deficit; the two are never added, and opening cash or committed funding is never subtracted twice. Each committed existing source appears exactly once on its own draw date. Unverified funding is a missing input, not a balancing plug.
- Model a proposed external facility separately from the measures above, after named owner/internal contributions, cost deferrals and reserve-policy choices have been modelled. Recompute cash, fees, interest and repayments with the request applied before any claim that it solves the requirement or any DSCR reported against it. A positive-cash business with a reserve-only shortfall may record an accepted reserve risk without a borrowing request, and still discloses the unchanged gap.
- Use synthetic fixtures for PM-10, MI-08 and SS-01/02/03 and for the financial and risk cases in F3-F7, F9, F11-F13 and F17. Prove the specification's two stated arithmetic cases: $100 opening cash against $150 expenditure yields a $50 cash deficit before reserves, not a negative requirement from subtracting the same $100 twice; and a later draw cannot fund an earlier payment. Retain F1's legacy $232.73 as historical evidence of the printed static method, not as a target for this dated sizing.

### Slice 2D implementation record

The standalone slice now provides `ScenarioShock`, `ScenarioDefinition`, `ScenarioCatalogue`, `ScenarioApplication`, `ShockReport`, `ReservePolicy`, `FundingMeasures`, `DebtService`, `LenderMetrics`, `ScenarioOutcome` and `ScenarioRunner`. Shocks are declared with their eligible targets and the defaults they came from; a shock with no stated targets, a cost-rate shock aimed at tax or financing, and a collection delay aimed at a payment are each rejected at construction rather than relied on by convention. Explicit shock periods are asserted through `ActualsWindow::assertForecastPeriods()` and fail on an elapsed reviewed month, while unbounded shocks cover the forecast window only. Volume shocks scale booked, delivered and invoiced units together and leave unknown counts unknown.

Funding measures read `C[s,t]` from the existing cash ledger's dated checkpoints rather than recomputing the walk, so verified opening cash and each committed source stay counted once on their own dates, unverified funding stays a missing input, and a later draw still cannot fund an earlier payment. Cash deficit and reserve-target shortfall are stored separately and never summed; a test asserts the sum is wrong so the double count cannot return quietly. Metrics return a status rather than a bare figure where the honest answer is not a number: a non-positive contribution has no finite break-even instead of zero units, a period with no debt service is Not applicable instead of zero or infinity, cash that never turns negative reports horizon coverage instead of an indefinite runway, and DSCR will not quote without its facility terms.

Requested and effective shock are reported separately by projecting base and shocked streams through the existing `RevenueProjection` and comparing usable base revenue, with the reasons they diverge recorded. A capacity-bound stream whose requested 0.8 changes nothing reports an effective factor of 1.0 and says so. Worst-case selection reads each definition's own named ranking metric, scenarios that cannot be ranked are listed rather than treated as the mildest case, and the maximum of each measure is recorded with the scenario and date that produced it. Nothing adjusts a figure to force monotonicity; a downside that improves an early cash minimum is reported as computed.

The 2A-2D engine is complete at 44 classes and 3,616 lines under `app/Services/Entrepreneurs/Forecast/`, and no file outside that directory references any of it. 2E is therefore the whole integration surface, and the slice table's single row covers five separable jobs: audited revision-checked persistence with explicit model selection, an input/review UI, nine new rule adapters, five existing rules extended onto the new ledger, and the proof obligations for Cannot verify, invalidation, bounded history and Draft-only routes.

Five of the nine new rules carry `Severity::Block`. Enabling them changes release verdicts for live entrepreneur clients, which is a production behaviour change rather than a refactor, so it does not belong in the same change that persists a 3,600-line engine for the first time. The Principal Advisor confirmed a four-pull-request split on 7 November 2026, with the rule activation isolated in the last and smallest of them.

| Pull request | Contents | Behaviour change |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 2E-1 | Versioned driver persistence, explicit model selection, snapshot capture of the selected version | None. No rule reads the new store |
| 2E-2 | The nine rule adapters and their fixtures, built and tested but not registered in `PhaseOneRules` | None. Adapters are unreachable from the evaluator |
| 2E-3 | Focused driver, actuals and reserve-policy input/review UI | Advisors can record version-2 inputs; verdicts still come from the legacy twenty rules |
| 2E-4 | Register the nine rules, extend MI-01/02/03/04 and SS-01 onto the new ledger | Catalogue goes from twenty to twenty-nine enabled rules for selected models only |

The Principal Advisor also confirmed per-client opt-in on the same date. A business plan with no selected version-2 model keeps today's twenty-rule verdict and today's legacy arithmetic unchanged; the new rules evaluate only where an advisor has explicitly saved and selected a reconstruction. This follows boundary note 8, which already states that no automatic conversion of stored budgets is proposed, and it means 2E-4 cannot silently alter an existing client's gate report.

### Slice 2E-1 implementation plan

This slice stores the version-2 driver model and records which model a plan uses. It enables no rule, renders no document differently and changes no existing verdict. `EntrepreneurBudget`, `BudgetCalculator` and the legacy twelve-value monthly editors are not modified; the new store sits beside them.

- Add `entrepreneur_forecast_models`, one row per business plan, holding the engine version, the typed driver payload, the reviewed actuals payload and the reserve policy. Store inputs only: this slice computes nothing, and an unwritten outputs column would invite a later slice to fill it from somewhere other than the engine. Follow the `entrepreneur_budgets` row-level-security shape, with the read scope covering the owning client, the assigned advisor and the entrepreneur. Unlike the legacy budget policy, the write scope includes the assigned advisor, because the reconstruction is an advisor decision rather than a founder entry; record that difference rather than leaving it implicit.
- Keep the store editable and revision-checked, not append-only. It is a working reconstruction, so `plan_gate_runs`-style immutability would be wrong; the audit trail comes from an `audit_events` row on every save, as the legacy budget service already does. Reuse the locked transaction, expected-revision assertion and change-fingerprint discipline in `EntrepreneurBudgetService::update()` rather than inventing a second concurrency story.
- Make selection explicit and separately audited. A saved model is not a selected model: selection is its own recorded act with its own actor and timestamp, and deselection is recorded too. A plan with no selected model is the per-client opt-in default and must be indistinguishable, in every downstream payload, from a plan that has no forecast model at all.
- Name the model version on the row and capture it in `BusinessPlanSnapshot::captureInputs()`, so a later slice can prove that snapshots and financial payloads share one version. Capture the version only; do not reintroduce derived release evaluation into input fingerprints, which boundary note 2 forbids.
- Type the stored payloads. The structural-boundary gate rejects `array<string,mixed>`, and a financial payload is exactly the place where an untyped bag would hide a missing value. Round-trip every payload through the existing engine value objects so a stored model cannot deserialize into something the engine would have refused to construct.
- Preserve missing values. A driver absent from the payload stays absent on read; it must never deserialize to zero, and a stored model that cannot be read back into the engine is a load failure, not a silently emptied reconstruction.
- Cover with PostgreSQL feature tests: save and reload, revision conflict rejection, an `audit_events` row for save, select and deselect, row-level-security scoping for each of the owning client, assigned advisor, entrepreneur and an unrelated advisor, and a regression asserting that an unselected or absent model leaves the existing budget, snapshot and gate payloads byte-identical.

### Slice 2E-1 implementation record

The store is `entrepreneur_forecast_models`, one row per business plan, holding the engine version, the driver payload, the reviewed actuals payload and the reserve policy. It carries no outputs column: this slice computes nothing, and an unwritten column would invite a later slice to fill it from somewhere other than the engine. Row-level security follows the `entrepreneur_budgets` shape, with the write scope widened to the assigned advisor because a version-2 reconstruction is an advisor decision rather than a founder entry; a test reads `pg_class` and `pg_policies` so the policy cannot quietly disappear from a later migration.

`ForecastModelStore` reuses the budget service's concurrency discipline rather than inventing a second one: a locked read, an optional expected revision the caller must still hold, and a revision bump only when the stored payload actually changed. Saving and selecting are separate recorded acts. A saved model changes nothing downstream, and a test asserts that saving one leaves the plan snapshot byte-identical; only selection makes a plan a version-2 plan, and deselection is recorded too rather than silently returning the plan to the legacy model. Audit events carry section hashes and the sections that moved rather than the payloads themselves, which are already recoverable from the row, and the event names the revision it produced rather than the one it replaced.

Decoding goes through the engine's own constructors, so a payload that was invalid on the way in cannot become valid by having been written to the database in between: a settlement with a direction its category does not allow is refused on load, as it was on construction. Absent stays absent. A driver that was never supplied loads as null and never as zero, an explicit zero stays an explicit zero, and an omitted nested object stays distinct from one stored empty. A present value of the wrong type, a missing required field and an unrecognised engine version are each load failures the advisor sees rather than a reconstruction that comes back thinner than it was saved. The stored reserve target is recomputed from the policy behind it, so a stored figure cannot drift away from its own basis.

### Slice 2E-2 implementation record

The nine phase-2 rules now have implementations: `RevenueRampRule` (PM-05), `StreamMembershipRule` (PM-06), `ProfitAllocationRule` (PM-08), `RunwayDefinitionRule` (PM-10), `SharedCapacityRule` (PM-11), `ElapsedActualsRule` (MI-07), `MonthThirteenBridgeRule` (MI-08), `StressDimensionRule` (SS-02) and `ScenarioFundingRule` (SS-03). None of them is registered in `PhaseOneRules`, which still returns twenty rules, so the evaluator continues to report all nine as not implemented and no verdict moves. A test asserts both halves of that: the implementations exist and the registered set does not contain them.

Each rule previously carried the placeholder input list `plan.claims, budget.facts, evidence.records`. Those are now the fact keys the implementations actually read, and a test asserts that every key a rule consumes in its passing fixture is declared in the catalogue, so an adapter cannot quietly read a fact the catalogue does not name. `RuleCatalogue::VERSION` moves with them, because the declared inputs are part of what a recorded risk decision was taken against. That invalidates prior risk acceptances and they must be taken again; this already happens on any deploy that touches `app/Services/Entrepreneurs`, since `SourceCapture::dependencies()` hashes every PHP file in that tree, and 2E-1 triggered it too.

Writing the tests found a defect in PM-06 worth recording, because it is the failure mode the rule exists to prevent. With one stream's membership flag missing, the first implementation left that stream out of the included-stream total and then reported the resulting shortfall against `model.base_revenue` as a mismatch: a confident Fail asserting a difference nobody had established. The total is now built from the model's own flags, a period containing any unresolved stream is Cannot verify rather than Fail, and plan-versus-model disagreement stays a separate finding. The same discipline holds across the nine: a missing unit-to-time conversion in PM-11 is unknown hours and never zero hours, an elapsed month with no record in MI-07 is Cannot verify and never an implied pre-trading month, and MI-08 returns Not applicable only with the scope it proved that from.

**These adapters read facts that nothing currently produces.** `GateContextFactory` assembles facts from `BudgetFacts` and `PlanFacts`, and neither emits `plan.ramp`, `capacity.available_hours`, `actuals.status`, `scenario.dimension` or the other keys introduced here. Registering the nine rules without a producer would return Cannot verify on all of them, five at `Severity::Block`, which would block every external release. 2E-4 must therefore ship the version-2 fact producer in the same change that registers the rules, and must gate it on the selected model so an unselected plan keeps today's twenty-rule report.

### Slice 2E-3 implementation record

`EntrepreneurForecastModelController` gives an advisor somewhere to build a version-2 reconstruction, at `entrepreneurs/{profile}/plans/{plan}/forecast-model`, reached from the founder-brief release panel beside the existing release-review link. Opening it needs `reviewBrief`; saving and selecting need `assess`, because a reconstruction is an advisor decision rather than a founder entry, which is the same reasoning behind the write scope widened in 2E-1.

The screen is deliberately partial. It captures the forecast calendar, opening cash, the reserve policy and reviewed actual months. Revenue streams, founder capacity, trade terms, owner pay steps, assets and tax are not edited here. **A save from this screen carries those sections through unchanged**, rebuilt from the stored model rather than from the form, because a form that posts only what it displays would silently delete every stream a later screen captures. A feature test seeds a stream through the store, saves through the HTTP form, and asserts the stream survives with its price intact.

What the screen does not capture, it reports. Every section is listed with whether it holds anything, what it holds, and whether this screen can edit it, so an advisor deciding whether to select a model can see the whole of it rather than the part that happens to have a form. Selecting an incomplete reconstruction is allowed and the page says what that means: the missing sections will read as Cannot verify, not as agreement. A reconstruction the engine refuses to decode is shown as the load failure it is, and selection of it is refused rather than recorded against a model nobody has read.

The reserve target is displayed, never typed. It is recomputed from the policy on every render, so a stated target cannot drift away from the basis behind it, and when the policy is incomplete the page names the inputs still needed instead of showing a number. Empty form boxes stay empty: an opening balance nobody has established is absent rather than zero, which is the same rule the codec enforces on load.

The engine's own constructors remain the validation of record. A value the engine refuses, such as a negative monthly fixed cost, is reported against the form and nothing is stored; a test asserts both the error and the absence of a saved row. Stale-revision conflicts and a repeated reporting month are refused the same way, and a refused save leaves the stored revision alone.

### Slice 2E-3b implementation record

This slice adds the editor for the two sections the phase-2 rules read most: revenue streams and founder capacity. They were chosen ahead of the other four because PM-05, PM-06, PM-11 and MI-08 all read stream and capacity facts, so without them the rule activation in 2E-4 would register rules that no advisor could ever supply data for.

The sections are posted in groups rather than as one form, at `POST .../forecast-model/drivers` beside the existing save route. Each group carries the sections it does not edit through unchanged, read back from the stored reconstruction rather than from the form. Recording founder hours therefore cannot discard a reserve policy somebody else recorded first, and a feature test asserts the calendar, opening cash, reserve policy and reviewed actuals all survive a driver save.

**The base-case flag is a three-state question, not a checkbox.** `RevenueStream::$inBaseCase` is a nullable boolean, and the distinction matters: PM-06 builds a base-revenue total from the streams the model flags as included, and an unflagged stream read as excluded would quietly drop out of that total and turn into a confident mismatch against `model.base_revenue`. The form asks "Counted in the base case" with "Not yet decided", "Yes" and "No, upside only", stores the unanswered case as absent, and the section summary reports how many streams have no flag. A feature test posts the unanswered state and asserts it is stored as null rather than as exclusion. This is the same defect found and fixed inside the PM-06 adapter in 2E-2, prevented here at the point of entry.

A stream's monthly schedule is stored as a sparse map keyed by month, because a month with no activity is genuinely absent rather than zero. The form matches that shape: an advisor adds the months that have activity instead of filling a grid of sixty. The payload sorts the map into a period-ordered list for display, so the months read in the order they occur whatever order they were written in.

Driver entry is refused until a calendar exists. A schedule of months only means something inside a horizon, so rather than invent one the screen says which step has to come first, and the form is withheld with that explanation until a reconstruction has been saved. Duplicate stream references, a month recorded twice within one stream, and a month recorded twice in founder capacity are each refused against the field that holds them, as are values the engine's own constructors reject. Every refused save leaves the stored revision alone.

Stream references are advisor-supplied and unique, not generated. Release findings are reported against the stream entity, so a reference that changes between saves would detach a finding from the stream it describes.

### Slice 2E-3c implementation record

This slice adds the editor for the last four input sections — trade terms, owner pay steps, assets, and tax policy and periods — at `POST .../forecast-model/terms`, the third posted group beside the calendar form and the driver form. The four share one form because they share a failure mode: each is a set of dated records whose identities and ordering the cash ledger relies on. The mapping lives in `AdvisorForecastTerms` rather than in the controller, so those invariants can be read in one place.

The ordering invariant is the substantive finding of the slice. Settlements become ledger movements — `ActualsWindow::outstandingMovements()` collects them from the trade items and hands them to `CashLedger` — and the ledger positions a movement by its date and sequence together, throwing on two movements in one position. A settlement's sequence therefore has to be unique per date across the whole set, not within its own trade item. Numbering each item's settlements from zero would have stored a reconstruction that the ledger refuses the moment it is read — the kind of defect that surfaces as an engine crash rather than as a finding. One counter runs across every settlement on the form, in the order they appear on screen, which makes the screen order the ledger order. A feature test records two items settling on the same day and asserts their positions differ.

A settlement's direction is derived, never asked for: cash moves in on a receivable and out on a payable, and `TradeItem` rejects any other pairing. That leaves the category, and only the categories the engine accepts in that direction are offered, with internal restricted transfers excluded outright because they settle nothing owed to or by anybody. Changing an item's kind clears any category that no longer suits it. An unchosen or unsuitable category is refused by name — "a receivable cannot settle as operating payment" — rather than by a constructor that can only report that the pair did not match.

Four more absences are kept distinct from answers, on the same reasoning as the base-case flag in 2E-3b. An item with no settlement rows stores `null` rather than an empty list, because `TradeBalances` reports absent terms as missing while an empty list is the different claim that the item settles with nothing. An asset classification left unanswered stays `null` rather than becoming "expense". A review or verification nobody has stated stays unstated rather than becoming a denial. And a tax policy with every field blank is stored as no policy at all, because the group posts all four sections together: constructing an empty `TaxPolicy` would have made a save of the trade terms report tax as captured when nothing about it had been said. Every yes/no question on the form posts `''`, `'yes'` or `'no'` rather than a boolean, because a boolean cannot carry the difference once an empty field has crossed the wire.

Owner pay steps couple a threshold to the revenue trigger: the engine refuses a base step that carries one. The form asks for a threshold only against the revenue trigger, and a value left behind by a changed trigger is dropped rather than reported as an error about something the advisor never restated.

Planning tax periods must meet end to end, because losses carry from one into the next and `TaxProjection` throws on a gap or an overlap. The store does not project, so a gapped set would have been stored silently and failed later; the gap is named at entry against the periods that hold it. Tax profit and adjustments are the only signed figures on the form, and the form says a loss is written with a leading minus.

Three gaps are reported rather than counted as captured, each grounded in what the engine does with them: an item whose settlement terms nobody has recorded, a settlement dated before the forecast opens (whose cash belongs in the opening balance), and an asset at or above 1,000 NZD whose classification nobody has reviewed, which is the threshold at which `AssetSchedule` stops producing a depreciation schedule. The tax summary names which policy fields are still blank, using the same list the projection reports as missing.

The section table gains a `bank_opening` row. `BankOpening` is held by every reconstruction and captured on no screen, so a table captioned "what this reconstruction holds" was overstating its own completeness. It is reported as not edited here; no editor is added for it. The unused `AdvisorForecastModelPayload::EDITABLE` constant, which this slice would have made both dead and wrong, is removed.

This slice still changes no verdict. A test evaluates the release gate before and after recording terms and selecting the model, asserts the serialised results are identical, and asserts the nine phase-2 rules remain among the seventeen deferred.

Local verification passed 28 forecast-model feature tests with 198 assertions and 14 page tests. PHPStan level 6, `tsc --noEmit`, ESLint, Prettier and Pint are clean, and all six structural gates pass against `origin/main`. Exact-commit CI results are recorded on the pull request.

Every input section now has an editor, so 2E-4 is no longer blocked on data entry. It remains blocked on the version-2 fact producer: the nine adapters read fact keys that neither `BudgetFacts` nor `PlanFacts` emits, and the three slices of 2E-3 have captured the inputs behind those keys without yet producing them.

### Slice 2E-4a implementation record

This slice set out to build the version-2 fact producer and found it blocked on something larger than a missing producer: **the version-2 forecast engine has no caller.** Every reference to `CashLedger`, `RevenueProjection`, `AssetSchedule`, `TaxProjection`, `ScenarioRunner` and the rest is inside the `Forecast` namespace itself. The components are complete and unit-tested, and nothing assembles them from a stored reconstruction. A fact producer has nothing to read until something runs the engine, so this slice builds that boundary and the next one produces facts from it.

`ForecastRun` is that boundary, and it is deliberately narrow. It runs the revenue projection, which needs nothing the stored model does not already hold, and reports the result as a verdict rather than a value: a projection that cannot run comes back as a run that failed, with the engine's own reason attached. The alternative is an engine exception surfacing on a screen an advisor is reading. Gaps are counted by reason with the number of months each reaches, because a sixty-month calendar missing one field would otherwise report that field sixty times and bury every other reason behind it.

The advisor screen now shows what the engine makes of the reconstruction, beside the table of what has been recorded into it. These answer different questions, and the distinction is the point: a complete-looking set of sections can still fail to project.

**A defect found and fixed.** The driver form validated the shape of a month but not its membership of the calendar, and `RevenueProjection` throws on a schedule that reaches past its horizon rather than discarding the month. A stream month or a founder-capacity month recorded beyond the horizon therefore stored a reconstruction that could not be projected at all. It was latent only because nothing ran the engine. The same state was reachable from the other side, by shortening the calendar under months already recorded, so both forms now refuse it and name the month and the horizon it falls outside. This is the same class as the tax-period gap caught in 2E-3c: a stored model that fails when projected rather than when saved.

**Three input gaps block the cash side**, found while reading the components this slice does not yet call:

- `OwnerPaySchedule::calculate` requires a first payment date and a source, and `ForecastModelInputs` holds neither.
- `TaxProjection::calculate` requires opening eligible losses, which the stored model does not hold.
- PM-08 reads `allocation.policy`, `allocation.amount`, `allocation.trigger`, `allocation.schedule` and `allocation.duplicate_line` for giving, reinvestment and owner distribution. No input section holds any of them, so PM-08 would report every policy missing however well the rest of the model is filled in.

The remainder of 2E-4 therefore runs: **2E-4b** the three missing inputs and the cash-side run; **2E-4c** capturing the selected reconstruction into the gate's source snapshot — `SourceCapture` records sections, the legacy budget, claims and evidence, and not the forecast model — together with the fact producer; **2E-4d** registering the nine rules, which is the first slice that moves a verdict and deserves to be the only thing in its own change; **2E-4e** extending MI-01 to MI-04 and SS-01 onto the new ledger.

This slice changes no verdict. It registers no rule and emits no fact; the seventeen deferred rules stay deferred. Adding a file under `app/Services/Entrepreneurs` changes the dependency hash in `SourceCapture`, which invalidates prior risk acceptances, as every slice touching that tree does.

### Slice 2E-4b implementation record

This slice runs the dated cash side of the stored reconstruction: the settlements, owner pay and asset purchases it holds, through `CashLedger` and the `FundingMeasures` that read it. It closes two of the three input gaps 2E-4a recorded and leaves the third, which belongs to a rule rather than to the cash run.

**Three inputs the engine needed and the stored model did not hold.** `OwnerPaySchedule` requires the date the first payment lands and the record the arrangement comes from, because every payment it generates carries that provenance into the ledger; the steps alone say what is paid and from when, not when the series starts. `TaxProjection` requires the losses carried into the first planning period, which sits beside the policy rather than inside it: a policy with losses eligible and nothing carried in is complete, not incomplete. All three are now captured on the terms form, stored as optional keys, and read back as absent on reconstructions saved before they existed — an older model is incomplete, which the run reports, not unreadable.

Because every form rebuilds the whole reconstruction, each new field had to be carried through the two forms that do not show it. A feature test records them on the terms form and then saves from both other forms in turn, asserting all three survive. This is the third time that carry-through has mattered, and the first time a field was added after the forms existed.

**The ordering invariant again, one level up.** 2E-3c numbered settlements across the form so two on one day could not claim the same ledger position. Assembling the full movement set reintroduces the same hazard between *sources*: settlements arrive carrying the numbers the trade form gave them, so owner pay and each asset are numbered above the highest of those rather than from zero. A settlement and an asset purchase landing on one day would otherwise collide and the ledger would refuse the whole set. A unit test puts both on 2026-02-10 and asserts the ledger positions them.

**Cash already in the opening balance is counted out rather than counted twice.** The ledger refuses a movement dated before the forecast opens, because that cash is in the opening position already. Settlements dated before the start are therefore excluded from the set handed to it and reported as a count, which is the figure the trade-terms section summary has been flagging since 2E-3c.

**Two gaps found by writing tests that were wrong.** A cash position came back unknown where a figure was expected: the fixture's only pay step waits on trailing revenue with no base step beneath it, so the weekly rate is unknown on every payment date, and the ledger will not resolve an unknown amount into a position. Then the reserve target came back unknown too — `FundingMeasures` withholds every figure when the ledger is incomplete, rather than publishing a measure drawn from a ledger with holes in it. Both are the engine being right and the test being wrong; both are now asserted as the contract they are. The advisor screen shows the two funding measures apart and says they are never added, because a cash deficit already sits inside the reserve shortfall.

Gap aggregation had to grow a second shape for this. A weekly pay series missing its rate reports the same gap on every payment date, which the per-reason counting introduced in 2E-4a does not collapse because the date is inside the reason string. Owner-pay gaps are now split back into reason and date, so one unknown rate reads as one gap with its reach; the ledger's own gaps are one-off and stay that way.

**What is left of 2E-4.** PM-08's allocation facts remain unheld by any input section — that is an editor, not a cash input, so it is a slice of its own rather than part of this one. Then **2E-4c** captures the selected reconstruction into the gate's source snapshot and builds the fact producer, **2E-4d** registers the nine rules, and **2E-4e** extends MI-01 to MI-04 and SS-01.

This slice changes no verdict. No rule is registered and no fact is emitted.

### Slice 2E-4c implementation record

This slice captures the selected reconstruction into the gate's source snapshot and produces release-gate facts from it. Facts are evidenced against the snapshot rather than against live state, so a gate run and a later re-reading of it see the same model, and the reconstruction is decoded back through the engine's own codec — a payload the engine would refuse produces no facts at all rather than a thinner set that reads as a complete model with gaps.

**Only once selected.** Saving a reconstruction is not choosing it, so a draft nobody has selected does not enter the snapshot. Were it captured, every revision of a draft would change the inputs hash and invalidate risk decisions taken against a model it has no bearing on. A feature test reads the gate context before and after selection and asserts the facts appear only after.

**Four keys are deliberately withheld, and the withholding is enforced.** `FactSet::one()` resolves competing records to nothing rather than to the first of them, and several of the keys the nine deferred rules read are already read by enabled ones. Emitting them now would move verdicts before a single new rule is registered:

| Key | Read by | Effect of emitting it now |
|---|---|---|
| `stream.revenue` | PM-04 (enabled) | PM-04 currently sees none; emitting would change its verdict |
| `scope.required_horizon` | MI-06 (enabled) | Would raise the snapshot's own required horizon |
| `scenario.lowest_cash` | SS-01 (enabled) | Would add scenarios SS-01 did not have |
| `model.external_request` | PM-12 (enabled) | A second record makes the lookup ambiguous, so PM-12 reports missing where it had a value |

They are listed in `ForecastModelFacts::WITHHELD`, a unit test asserts none is emitted, and a feature test asserts a selected model adds no competing record for any of them. They belong to the slice that registers the nine rules, where the interaction can be designed and tested as one change. The three existing "changes no release verdict" tests — each of which selects a model and compares the full serialised results — pass unchanged, which is the empirical proof that the analysis holds.

**What the producer emits.** Periods follow the `month:N` convention the rest of the gate uses, counted from the model's own start, so a fact produced here can be compared with one produced from the stored budget. Every fact carries the captured record it came from; a unit test asserts that of all of them, because a fact without its source is an assertion rather than evidence.

Two judgements inside it are worth naming. A stream nobody has classified emits no membership fact at all — the rules require a boolean, and an absent flag reported as missing is the finding, while a flag invented here would be the defect. And a stream whose provider changes from month to month emits no single provider, because naming one of them would be a claim nobody made.

A test that expected a base revenue total found none, and the engine was right: one stream in that model is unclassified, so there is no total to publish. The three-state flag recorded on the advisor screen in 2E-3b reaches the fact as an absent figure rather than as a total that quietly leaves the unclassified stream out. That chain is now asserted end to end.

**Inventory of the nine rules' remaining facts.** Rather than discover these one slice at a time, here is the whole picture:

- **MI-07** is fully fed.
- **PM-05** and **PM-06** have their model side fed. Their `plan.*` keys are the document's own claims and belong to `PlanFacts`, not here; PM-06 also needs the withheld `stream.revenue`.
- **PM-11** is fed except `provider.capacity_hours` and `provider.start`. `StreamMonth` records additional capacity in units, not the provider's hours, and holds no start date for a provider — **two new inputs**.

### Slice 2E-4e implementation record

This slice puts MI-01 onto the reconstruction's dated cash ledger. **Of the five rules the slice was scoped to extend, one could be extended and four cannot**, for reasons recorded below; the four are blocked on inputs or engine work rather than on this slice's design.

**MI-01 could not reach a resolved verdict on any plan before this.** Nothing on the platform produced a ledger entry: `ledger.amount`, `ledger.category`, `ledger.date`, `ledger.source`, `ledger.coverage` and `cash.period_start` had no producer at all, so the rule reported every month's ledger as missing however much the stored budget said about the balances, and Cannot verify was the ceiling. A reconstruction holds exactly what it was waiting for, and on a complete one inside the plan's horizon MI-01 now reaches **Pass**, with the month-to-month continuity, the annual totals and the per-entry date, identity and sign checks all reconciling against classified lines.

`ForecastRun::cash()` carries three things through that it previously dropped: each month's receipts and payments, and the positioned movements themselves. The month's net movement is the ledger's own arithmetic rather than a difference taken back off the two balances, which would re-state the ledger instead of reading it.

**The two category vocabularies do not line up, and the direction decides as much as the category.** The rule holds ledger amounts non-negative and takes direction from the classification, so every inflow has to reach an inflow category or the classified lines would not sum to the month's reported movement. Interest, fees and owner pay become operating payments: the rule names none of them, a distribution is separately named, and cash paid out for the owner's work is an operating payment rather than a return of capital.

Four combinations have no honest home and get **no category at all**, which has MI-01 report the entry rather than reconcile it. A tax refund, a GST refund and asset disposal proceeds are receipts, but the rule's three inflow categories are customer receipts, other operating receipts and financing inflows, and none of them describes a refund of tax or the proceeds of a disposal without asserting something nobody recorded. A restricted transfer is a movement between restricted and unrestricted cash, which the categories do not cover. Filing any of them under a category that does not fit would let a month reconcile on a classification the gate's own taxonomy cannot support. **This is a gap in the rule's category list, not in the engine**, and extending that list is spec work rather than something to paper over here.

`ledger.source` carries the record and the movement within it. One invoice can settle in instalments, and the rule requires each ledger line to be identifiable once, so the record on its own would read as the same line counted twice — a false Fail on an ordinary deposit-and-balance invoice.

**Precedence, because a selected reconstruction is the plan financial model.** `FactSet::one()` resolves competing records to nothing, so a producer emitting a key the budget already emits turns a readable figure into a missing one. `ForecastModelFacts::SUPERSEDES` names the keys the reconstruction takes over, and `GateContextFactory` applies it only when the producer actually emitted something — a model the engine cannot read displaces nothing. Two keys are taken over without being replaced, both also in `WITHHELD`: `model.available_funds`, because the budget derives it as its own opening plus its own funding and the sum becomes a mixture of two models once the opening term changes, and the reconstruction holds no funding inventory to state one from; and `scenario.lowest_cash`, because SS-01 compares it against the minimum of the month-end closings while the ledger's own lowest cash is the balance after each individual movement and can dip below every month end. Publishing either figure under that key would misstate one of the two measures. `model.cash_deficit` and `model.reserve_gap` come off the same replaced curve and are deliberately left alone: PM-12 tests them against the plan's own funding claim rather than against any curve, so they still stand on their own terms.

**The takeover costs SS-01 the scenarios it reconciles today, and that was the hard call.** The first design scoped it to the base scenario so the budget's scenario curves survived. The canary caught the consequence: `CashLedgerRule::scenario()` checks every scenario's first month against `model.opening_cash`, which is one figure with no scenario of its own, so each budget scenario started from a balance the plan's financial model no longer uses and SS-01 moved from Cannot verify to **Fail**. A scenario curve is built off the base curve the reconstruction replaced, so the honest resolution is that the reconstruction owns the curve in every scenario and SS-01 reports the scenarios as not established. A check that passes against the model a plan has stopped using is worth less than an honest gap, and SS-02 and SS-03 already say the scenario dimension is unestablished. The banner on the advisor screen now says this, alongside what the ledger check gains.

**The horizon stays the plan's, not the model's.** `model.horizon` is not taken over: how many months a plan must forecast is a property of its scope, and selecting a reconstruction does not restate it. Two consequences are asserted rather than left to chance. A plan with no budget has no stated horizon, so MI-01 reports that and never reaches the ledger — correct, and the reason the three earlier verdict canaries were silent about MI-01. And a reconstruction reaching past the plan's horizon has cash movements outside the evaluated window, which MI-01 demonstrates as a Fail; the advisor resolves it by aligning the two. That is the one cross-model edge this slice leaves open, and a test pins it.

### Slice 2E-4f implementation record

This slice adds the funding-source section, the first of the two input sections the 2E-4e record named as blockers. MI-02 had no funding inventory to read: the reconstruction held none, and funding reached cash only as a trade settlement categorised `funding`, carrying no identity, commitment, restriction, availability or draw schedule. It now has one, and MI-02 goes from every subcheck missing to exactly one.

**Funding is kept apart from opening cash, and the form enforces it.** Opening cash answers what the business already holds; a funding source answers what it has arranged to receive. A draw dated before the forecast opens is already inside the opening balance, so counting it as funding too counts the same money twice — which is the first thing MI-02's available-funds check looks for. A draw dated after the forecast ends never arrives inside the horizon, so it is not funding this forecast can spend. Both are refused at the point of entry with messages that name the source and say which of the two mistakes it is, rather than left to surface as a reconciliation failure nobody can act on. `FundingSource` separately refuses a draw dated before its own availability, as `TaxPeriod` refuses a period ending before it starts: that pair is not a finding about the forecast, it is a record that does not describe anything.

**No total until somebody says the list is finished.** `funding.inventory_complete` cannot be read off the row count: an empty list is equally a business with no funding and a section nobody has filled in, and only somebody who looked can tell the two apart. It is a three-state question stored absent until answered. `model.funding_total` and `model.available_funds` are published only when the inventory is declared complete *and* every source carries an amount — the sum of a partial list is not a total, and one unpriced source withholds the figure the others would imply rather than quietly understating it. A unit test asserts each of those three states.

`model.available_funds` therefore leaves `WITHHELD`, where 2E-4e put it because the reconstruction had no inventory to state it from. `model.funding_total` and `funding.amount` join `SUPERSEDES`: the reconstruction's inventory is the plan's, the budget's named sources belong to the model it replaced, and a rule iterating both would walk two models' funding at once. This reverses the note in 2E-4e that said the budget's funding was left in place because the reconstruction was silent about funding. It is no longer silent.

**What MI-02 has left is not a funding gap.** With the inventory recorded, the named source, its dates, its commitment, its restriction and the draw matching it in the financing ledger all reconcile. One subcheck remains unresolved: `opening-provenance`, which wants opening cash by account and restriction scope. The reconstruction holds that in `BankOpening`, which no screen captures yet, so the rule stays Cannot verify and says precisely why. A feature test asserts the unresolved set is exactly `['opening-provenance']`, so the day that section lands, the test says whether MI-02 resolves.

**The verdict canary was widened, with its reasons written down.** `FED_BY_THE_RECONSTRUCTION` now names ten rules: the six phase-2 rules, MI-01 for the cash ledger, MI-02 for the funding inventory, PM-12 for the funding checks it copies from MI-02, and SS-01 for the scenarios it loses. ### Slice 2E-4g implementation record

This slice adds the fixed-cost section, the second of the two input sections the 2E-4e record named as blockers, and the one SS-01's profit decomposition also waits on. MI-03 had nothing to read: trade items are individual dated payables rather than rate-times-cadence arrangements, so no producer on the platform emitted `cost.rate`, `cost.quantity`, `cost.frequency`, `cost.expense`, `cost.payment` or `model.fixed_cost`, and the rule could report its whole schedule missing and go no further. It now reads a recorded schedule and **passes**.

The engine already held `ExpenseSchedule`, built for exactly this and with no caller, the same state the rest of the engine was in before 2E. This slice is largely its boundary.

**Recognition and payment are separate answers, and the accrual between them is a position rather than a discrepancy.** A fixed cost relates to the period it covers, so it is recognised straight-line at its annualised rate; the invoices fall whenever the arrangement says they fall. An annual premium invoiced once in March is an expense of one twelfth every month, not a spike in March, and the gap between the two in a month is the accrual or prepayment the cost carries. Reporting them as one figure would hide a premium paid in advance. The invoices themselves go into the cash ledger beside the trade payables, because recurring overheads are real cash leaving the business and a curve without them understates what the business spends.

**The schedule is held unrounded, which is what the rule asks for in so many words.** Its missing-subcheck message names "unrounded" rates, quantities, frequencies and monthly schedules, and the rule catalogue says the same. This is not a stylistic choice: `ExpenseSchedule`'s weighted recognition allocates an exact total and therefore has to put the cent residual somewhere, measured at 83.37 against a rate of 83.3333 in the final month of an annual charge of 1,000 — past the rule's one-cent tolerance, and growing with the horizon to as much as 59 cents on a five-year model. Monthly charges divide cleanly; annual and quarterly ones usually do not, so this would have been a systematic false Fail rather than a corner case. Recognising at the unrounded annualised rate avoids it without weakening anything: an invoice window that does not cover the calendar at the stated cadence still diverges, and still shows up — in the cash ledger and in a growing accrual, which is where a wrong creditor term or invoice window actually belongs.

**The two display keys are deliberately not emitted, and are in `WITHHELD` with their reason.** The budget already states both, from two genuinely independent places: a trace summed over its own cost rows, and the monthly fixed cost its computation reports. That independence is the whole value of the rule's whole-dollar comparison of them. A third record under either key would resolve both to nothing and take a working check with it, and a reconstruction publishing one figure under both would have manufactured a check that passes because it was handed the same number twice.

**What MI-03 says about an annually billed cost is a gap in the rule, not in this producer.** The rule requires dated payments against every cost in every month, and an annual premium has cash in one of them; the other eleven report as missing rather than as nothing owed, even though the recognised expense and the accrual reconcile exactly. A feature test pins that behaviour with the exact count rather than engineering around it. **[NEEDS DECISION]** whether `payments:N` should accept a month whose cash is legitimately zero when the accrual bridge accounts for the whole expense.

Two cadences are deliberately not offered. Fortnightly is a cadence the gate can annualise but `ForecastDates::recur` cannot generate, and `once` is a single charge, which is a trade item rather than a fixed cost. Offering either would store an arrangement that refuses the moment it is projected. Adding fortnightly recurrence to a shared engine primitive is a change outside this slice.

**The canary was not widened, which is the point.** MI-03 is kept out of `FED_BY_THE_RECONSTRUCTION`, because that list excuses a rule from the byte-identical comparison and the existing canaries select a reconstruction carrying no costs — where MI-03 genuinely cannot move. A costed canary sits beside them instead: with a schedule recorded, exactly `MI-01`, `MI-03` and `MI-07` change verdict, and every rule outside the set this reconstruction may touch is compared whole, so a rule quietly reading a cost fact without changing its mind would still be caught.

### Slice 2E-4h implementation record

MI-04 wants a monthly chain of profit, adjustments, tax expense and carried losses. Nothing stated one. The stored tax periods hold a period's profit, which is the form a return is filed in, and the 2E-4e record named the blocker precisely: splitting a period figure into twelve months invents eleven of them. This slice gives the rule a real monthly chain over the months where monthly profit is a recorded figure, and says plainly why it stops there.

**Monthly profit comes from the reviewed months, because that is the only place it is recorded.** `ActualProfit` has defined the shape of a reviewed month's profit and loss since the engine was written, and the actuals form filled it with seven nulls. It now captures it: revenue, operating expenses, depreciation, interest, other income, profit before tax and income tax expense, signed, so a reviewed loss is written as one. `tax.profit` is read from `profit_before_tax`, and only where the month is reconciled — a profit figure nobody reconciled would otherwise head a chain every later month depends on.

**`MonthlyTaxChain` is the monthly form of the policy `TaxProjection` applies to periods.** A loss raises the carried balance and waits for profit to absorb it; nothing is refunded for it. A month whose taxable profit cannot be established stops the chain rather than being read as a zero, because every month after it would otherwise carry a balance computed from a figure nobody stated. Where the policy says losses are not eligible, a loss is simply not carried — and still not refunded.

**A period's adjustments are recognised in the month that period ends.** An adjustment is determined when the return is prepared, so the months before it carry none and the period total is exact either way. That places the timing, not the amounts, which remain the advisor's. A calendar month inside no declared period carries no adjustment at all, and the chain reports it rather than assuming zero.

**[NEEDS DECISION] Forecast months have no profit, and the reason is a modelling question rather than a missing field.** Deriving one means deciding whether a receivable trade item is the same revenue a stream already counted, and whether a payable is the same cost the fixed-cost schedule already recognises. The reconstruction records revenue in two places and costs in two places with nothing tying them together, so a derived profit could double-count in either direction. A tax figure is a poor place to take that decision quietly, so the chain stops where the reviewed months stop and MI-04 reports the horizon as uncovered — which is the truthful reading. Resolving this is also what SS-01's per-scenario profit decomposition waits on, so it is one decision for both.

`RevenueProjection` already computed `base_contractor_cost` beside `base_revenue` and `ForecastRun` dropped it; it is now carried through, because a decomposition needs both halves and deriving one from the other would state a margin nobody recorded. That is groundwork for the decision above, not a use of it.

As with MI-03's `expense:N`, the rule's `expense:N` arithmetic is a self-check for a derived producer: the chain computes expense and carried losses, and the rule recomputes them. The load-bearing parts are the policy preconditions, `coverage`, and the cash-tax checks, which remain unresolved until a tax cash plan is recorded. `TaxCashPlan` is a further orphan — defined, unit-tested, and held by no input — so `cash-tax:N` and `cash-ledger:N` stay missing and are the next slice in this line.

### Slice 2E-4i implementation record

This completes MI-04's cash side. The 2E-4h record named `TaxCashPlan` as a further orphan — defined, unit-tested, and held by no input — so `cash-tax:N` and `cash-ledger:N` stayed missing however well the chain reconciled. The reconstruction now holds one, and MI-04's unresolved set for a reviewed-months model is exactly `['coverage']`.

**The schedule is asked for as a position before it is asked for as rows.** An empty tax schedule is three different answers: nothing is due, the business is outside the regime, or nobody has worked it out yet. Only the first two are finished, and no row count distinguishes them, so the scope state is captured for income tax and GST separately and a schedule with rows against a `none_due` or `not_applicable` scope is refused at entry naming which of the two is at odds. Where the scope is unstated the producer emits nothing at all: a month cannot state when its tax is paid on the strength of a schedule nobody has scoped.

**Only the payable movement is derived.** A month's tax can be recognised long before it is paid, so the payment is read from the schedule and the payable is the difference. Deriving the payment from the expense would state a payment date nobody scheduled. A month with nothing scheduled paid nothing, which is a position rather than a gap — the expense it recognised is carried as tax payable until a later month settles it — so it states a nil payment dated to the month it belongs to, rather than reporting the month unestablished.

**`cash-ledger:N` is the check worth having here.** It compares the scheduled payment against the `cash_tax` lines in the ledger, and those lines come from two places: this schedule, and any trade settlement an advisor categorised as tax. Recording a tax payment in both — which is the easy mistake, since the trade form offers a tax category — makes the ledger exceed the schedule and the check reports it. That is a genuine reconciliation rather than a restatement of one producer's arithmetic.

**A month carrying a refund states no payment at all.** The ledger gives a tax refund no gate category, for the reason recorded in 2E-4e: the rule's three inflow categories are customer receipts, other operating receipts and financing inflows, and none describes a refund of tax without asserting something about its nature. A net payment figure could therefore never agree with the ledger it is checked against, so the month is reported unestablished instead. That is the honest outcome, and it names the entry to the advisor rather than reconciling on a classification the taxonomy cannot support.

Tax paid before the forecast opens is refused at the form, as funding draws and fixed-cost invoices already are: the ledger refuses to position it, and it belongs in the opening balance.

What is left on MI-04 is `coverage`, and that is the open decision from 2E-4h — forecast months have no profit, so a three-month reviewed chain is not a twelve-month tax schedule. Nothing in this slice changes that, and nothing in it should.
